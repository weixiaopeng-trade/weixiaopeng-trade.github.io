Trade intermediaries as an absorber of unanticipated financial shock: The case of delay in export tax rebates in China

Abstract

This paper shows how intermediaries insure exporters against financing shocks. I develop a global-sourcing model in which firms choose between direct exporting and selling via intermediaries while facing two export-policy components: anticipated rule shocks in statutory terms and unanticipated implementation uncertainty—including lags, delays, and administrative discretion. Implementation uncertainty raises the effective cost of direct exporting by lengthening firms’ cash cycles, while intermediation shortens that cycle and shifts the uncertainty from producers to intermediaries, providing contract-free insurance. The model predicts substitution toward intermediation when implementation uncertainty rise and export–import complementarity. I then bring the framework to data using China’s export-rebate regime 2007–2013 as a case study. Linking customs transactions, firm tax ledgers, and product–year schedules, I measure exposure to rule shocks and implementation uncertainty. Both depress trade, with imports reacting more strongly. Consistent with the model, intermediation substantially attenuates timing uncertainty—roughly halving its effect on trade margins—while offering little protection against rule changes.

Trade Intermediation and Manufacturers’ Product Scope in China

Abstract

Can the way manufacturers reach foreign buyers affect what they produce? Using Chinese industrial-survey, Customs, and physical-output records for 2000–2006, we examine how intermediary activity on manufacturers’ pre-existing export routes relates to production scope. Greater exposure is associated with a broader product range. The response is concentrated among established products outside manufacturers’ initial direct Customs declarations; these products may also have been sold domestically. New-product entry shows no comparable response. These findings link export-market organization to manufacturers’ product portfolios, while not identifying an effect of WTO accession or any specific reform.
